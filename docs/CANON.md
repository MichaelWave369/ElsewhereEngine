# Elsewhere Engine: public canon charter (v0.1)

> The universe broke into stories. The stories forgot they were one.

## Foundational idea

Worlds are not disposable genre skins. Each is a lived civilization with its own causal habits, history, ethics and inhabitants. **Genre becomes geology**: comedy, horror, fantasy, noir, and science fiction may behave like local laws of experience. Travelers discover contradictions, artifacts and echoes that challenge those laws.

No single narrator, institution or realm currently possesses an authoritative explanation for the whole cosmology. Mysteries must support multiple hypotheses until an earned reveal.

## Publicly known anchors

- **The Crossroads:** a place of impossible transit and the setting for the initial playable episode.
- **Department of Everything (D.O.E.):** an absurdly serious institution claiming responsibility for maintaining reality. Its claims must not be mistaken for proof of authority.
- **The Archive of Impossibilities:** a catalog of anomalous objects, reports and unresolved incidents. Its custodians favor paperwork over understanding.
- **Elevator 13:** a prohibited route that sometimes appears and never points the same direction twice.
- **The fracture:** a name for the apparent separation of realities. Whether there ever was one unified reality is an open question.

## Coherent magic, not a convenient cheat code

Magic is treated as **a negotiated exception to the local laws**, not an unlimited superpower. In-world witnesses debate whether it is supernatural, misunderstood physics, or something more fundamental.

1. **Anchor:** each working needs a binding object, promise, location, memory, name or relationship.
2. **Cost:** a persistent debt or alteration is incurred; costs must be legible before the player commits.
3. **Intent:** what is desired matters, but intent does not override all constraints.
4. **Consequences:** powerful changes leave traces that can be investigated later.
5. **Limits:** no spell can undo every loss, and the same rule must apply to protagonists and antagonists.

## Tonal constitution

A horrifying mystery must be allowed to stay horrifying. Comedy emerges from character, bureaucracy and human absurdity, not from mocking grief or annihilating stakes. The Department may be funny; the people caught in its machinery still matter.

## Mystery integrity

- Distinguish what a character believes from what the story has verified.
- Keep clues replayable and discoverable; no puzzle depends on random guessing.
- Never publish late-game revelations in public issue titles, changelogs, commits or marketing.
- The eventual truth can recontextualize prior clues but should not contradict evidence without an in-world cause.
- Avoid direct use of third-party characters, places, dialogue, logos, music, storylines or trademarked elements.

## Crossover rule

Other games join by explicit opt-in **canon bridge** documents. A bridge cannot retcon another game's established events, expose its secret endings, or read/write its saves without a separately reviewed migration and user consent. Crossovers are narrative possibilities, **not a claim of implemented engine integration**.
